# Novelty check: symmetric-subspace (weak Schur) test, one-vs-two sources, unknown pointing
Method: WebSearch + WebFetch. [abstract] = abstract page / search snippet only; [partial text] = part of full text read. NOT read: Harrow-Montanaro, O'Donnell-Wright, Montanaro-de Wolf full texts; Stanisic-Turner full text; last 13.7k chars of 2110.00986.

## A. Super-resolution / one-vs-two
1. de Almeida, Lewenstein, Skotiniotis, "Superresolving collective quantum measurements", arXiv:2110.00986 [partial text: ~100k of 113k chars via HTML v5]. (NB: the ID in the brief is this paper, not the "misalignment" one.) N copies of the two-source state, weak Schur sampling: J^2 distribution depends only on purity -> purity estimate, robust to misalignment because it uses only permutation symmetry; then a within-J measurement for relative intensity. N=2 equals HOM. Task = ESTIMATION of separation and relative intensity, saturating multiparameter QCRB asymptotically. No hypothesis testing, no one-vs-two discrimination, no error exponents (per my read). Needs quantum memory (spin ensembles etc.); Schur transform poly gates. Overlap: PARTLY - same machinery (weak Schur sampling of stored photons, misalignment robustness, purity as the signature) but different task; the pure-vs-mixed test with exponent -log(lambda1) and zero background is not stated.
2. de Almeida, Kolodynski, Hirche, Lewenstein, Skotiniotis, "Discrimination and estimation of incoherent sources under misalignment", arXiv:2003.01166 [abstract + search snippets]. SPADE-type demultiplexing with misalignment; tasks: separation estimation and 1-vs-2 discrimination; error probability vs misalignment, numerics for long interrogation. No HOM/symmetric projection in abstract. Overlap: NO for method; YES as the problem setting (1-vs-2 under misalignment, single-photon mode sorting = the "classical" baseline class we beat).
3. Parniak et al., "Beating the Rayleigh limit using two-photon interference", PRL 121, 250503 (2018), arXiv:1803.07096 [partial text: abstract + first paragraphs; keyword counts: 'purity' 0, 'mixed' 0, 'discriminat' 0, 'Schur' 0, 'symmetric' 2]. HOM interference of a pair of emitters' photons followed by spatially resolved detection; ESTIMATION of separation and centroid. Overlap: NO/PARTLY (HOM for superresolution, but estimation, no 1-vs-2 test, no exponent).
4. Muratore, Triggiani, Tamma, arXiv:2412.10057 (PRApplied 23, 054033) [abstract]. Reference photon interferes with a photon from two displaced thermal sources; transverse-momentum sampling; separation ESTIMATION within factor 2 of quantum limit. Search snippet there notes storing two thermal photons is demanding. Overlap: PARTLY (reference-photon variant resembles our kappa-calibration photons, k=1 reference) but estimation only.
5. Muratore & Tamma, arXiv:2602.08578 [abstract]: time-delay analog; estimation. NO.
6. Lu, Krovi, Nair, Guha, Shapiro, "Quantum-optimal detection of one-versus-two incoherent optical sources with arbitrary separation", arXiv:1802.02300 (npj QI 4, 64) [abstract]. Quantum Chernoff bound for 1-vs-2, KNOWN CENTROID; B-SPADE and SLIVER achieve it, no joint measurements. This is the benchmark a0^2 in our claim. Overlap: NO (no unknown pointing / no symmetric projection) but it is the reference bound.
7. Zanforlin et al., "Optical quantum super-resolution imaging and hypothesis testing", Nat. Commun. 13, 5373 (2022), arXiv:2202.09406 [search snippet only]: 1-vs-2 hypothesis test with interferometer, false-negative minimisation. Overlap: NO for method.

## B. Property testing
- Harrow-Montanaro product test (FOCS 2010) [search snippets only]: two-copy symmetric-projection test for product states (pure); acceptance 1-Theta(eps). Not an exponent result for pure-vs-mixed.
- O'Donnell-Wright (arXiv:1501.05028) [snippets]: purity/max-mixed testing, Theta(d/eps^2) copies; EYD (= weak Schur sampling) algorithm. Sample-complexity regime, not fixed-spectrum error exponent.
- Quantum state certification (arXiv:1708.06002) [snippet]: mixed-state certification is hard in general.
- I did NOT verify whether -log(lambda_max) for symmetric-projection pure-vs-rank-2 testing is stated, or optimal, anywhere. Elementary fact (acceptance prob = tr[rho^{(x)k} P_sym] = h_k(spectrum)) is textbook; optimality for (unknown pure) vs (fixed-spectrum mixed) is NOT FOUND in what I read.

## C. Linear-optical implementations
- Stanisic & Turner, "Discriminating distinguishability", arXiv:1806.01236, PRA 98, 043839 [abstract]: HOM as mixed-state discrimination; representation-theoretic; projection onto irreps of U(m); up to 9 photons in 9 modes; QFT suboptimal for fully distinguishable. Controlled vs uncontrolled DOF (internal). Overlap: PARTLY (same mathematical object, symmetric-vs-nonsymmetric irreps for photons), not applied to transverse modes/imaging. 'Schur sampling' not in abstract.
- Moylett & Turner arXiv:1803.03657 [snippet]: Schur transform circuit for partially distinguishable bosons. Not imaging.
- 2110.00986 itself: N<5 spectral decomposition via multi-core fibres + post-processing (so a linear-optics route for small k is already suggested there).

## D. Programmable discriminators
- Bergou & Hillery, PRL 94, 160501 (2005), quant-ph/0504201 [abstract]: unambiguous discrimination of unknown data state against two program states; universal. Bergou et al. quant-ph/0602164 (n data copies), He-Bergou-Ren 0706.2429, 1007.5497 (multi-copy, symmetric-subspace projectors) [snippets]. Overlap: PARTLY for our kappa-reference variant (symmetric projection of data with reference copies is the standard device), but task is discrimination of two unknown/reference qubit states, not photon-pointing-invariant imaging, and no exponents read.

## Verdicts
(i) 1-vs-2 symmetric-projection test under unknown pointing: PARTLY KNOWN. Weak Schur sampling of stored photons for superresolution under unknown centroid/misalignment exists (de Almeida et al. 2110.00986) with purity as the key statistic, and N=2 is HOM; but for ESTIMATION. A hypothesis-testing/Chernoff-exponent treatment of 1-vs-2 was NOT FOUND.
(ii) Exponent (-log lambda1, ratio ~0.98-0.999 of a0^2, zero background, any crosstalk): NOT FOUND in what I read; near-optimality claim vs known-centroid QCB (1802.02300) is our own. Hedge: sources I could only read as abstracts may contain related statements.
(iii) Comparison with classical centroid-estimate + mode-sorting learners (1.6-5.7x): NOT FOUND; 2003.01166 is closest baseline (misalignment degrades SPADE discrimination).
(iv) Implementation: quantum memory + Schur transform (2110.00986 proposes spin ensembles, poly-gate Schur transform), small k via multiport interferometer/multicore fibre; Stanisic-Turner for photonic irrep projections; Muratore et al. need a reference photon + thermal photon interference.
Suggested residual checks: full text of 2110.00986 tail and 2003.01166; O'Donnell-Wright; Stanisic-Turner full text; Zanforlin 2202.09406; search "Schur sampling hypothesis testing Chernoff exponent".
